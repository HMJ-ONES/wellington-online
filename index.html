How Wellington works

  • Goal: lowest score wins. Numbered cards are worth 10, Jack & Queen 11, Joker 0, King −1, Ace −2. (Points are editable — see Rules.)
  • Start: everyone gets 4 cards face-down. Peek at any 2 of your own once, then put them back.
  • Cards on your table are always face-down. You only ever see a card while you are actively looking at it (peek / 5 / 6 / 8 / the card you just drew) — then it goes back face-down. A small gold dot marks cards you have seen.
  • Your turn: draw the top card and look at it. Either swap it blind for one of your table cards (the old card goes face-up in the middle) or discard the drawn card face-up. Exactly one discard per turn.
  • Card effects trigger ONLY on a normal turn discard (the drawn card you discard, or the table card you swap out): 5 peek 1 of your own, 6 peek 1 of another player's, 7 blind-swap 1 of yours with 1 of theirs, 8 look at both cards, then choose whether to swap them. Exactly one effect per turn discard. Snapped cards never trigger effects — not even a snapped 5, 6, 7 or 8.
  • Snap! Whenever a card lands in the middle, anyone may discard a card of the same rank from their own table before the next card lands there — and if you hold two of that rank, you may snap twice in a row in the same window. Wrong rank? That card is shown, goes back, and you must take a penalty card you can't discard right away — it becomes a normal card from your next turn on. Advanced: when setting up a room (or a bot game) you can turn Snap tips off — no banner at the top, just watch the middle yourself. Snapping still works exactly the same.
  • WELLINGTON: on your turn, before drawing, hit the big button when you think you're lowest. Everyone else gets exactly one last turn, then all cards are revealed. Only one call per game — and nobody may touch the caller's cards during the final round.
  • Deck runs out? The discard pile (except its top card) is reshuffled into a new draw pile.
