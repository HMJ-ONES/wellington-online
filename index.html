How Wellington works

  • Goal: lowest score wins. Numbered cards are worth their face value (2 is 2, 10 is 10), Jack & Queen 11, Joker −2, King −1, Ace 0. (Points are editable — see Rules.)
  • Start: everyone gets 4 cards face-down. Peek at any 2 of your own once, then tap I'm Ready — everyone peeks and readies at the same time.
  • Cards on your table are always face-down. You only ever see a card while you are actively looking at it (peek / 5 / 6 / 8 / the card you just drew) — then it goes back face-down, with no mark of any kind. A card you have seen looks exactly like one you haven't — remember on your own.
  • Your turn: draw the top card and look at it. Either swap it blind for one of your table cards (the old card goes face-up in the middle) or discard the drawn card face-up. Exactly one discard per turn.
  • Card effects trigger ONLY on a normal turn discard (the drawn card you discard, or the table card you swap out): 5 peek 1 of your own, 6 peek 1 of another player's, 7 blind-swap 1 of yours with 1 of theirs, 8 look at both cards, then choose whether to swap them. Exactly one effect per turn discard. The effect is offered first, never fired at once — a blinking bar below the table asks "Use card effect? Yes / No" while the snap window stays open. Snapped cards never trigger effects — not even a snapped 5, 6, 7 or 8.
  • Turn timer: every turn lasts at most 60 seconds — the ⏱ End Turn button shows the countdown. You must draw a card and discard something before ending your turn (the button is greyed out until you have drawn); when the clock runs out, the turn ends on its own.
  • Snap! Whenever a card lands in the middle, anyone may discard a card of the same rank — from their own table or any other player's — before the next card lands there. Hold and drag a matching card to the middle to snap (clicking never snaps, so a stray tap can't cost you a penalty card). If you hold two of that rank, you may snap twice in a row in the same window. Snapping someone's last card ends the game at once — they win with 0. Snapped someone else's card? You choose: give them one of your cards, or let them draw a new one from the deck. The Wellington caller's cards are protected in the final round. Wrong rank? That card is shown, goes back to its owner, and you must take a penalty card you can't discard right away — it becomes a normal card from your next turn on (so if you had 4 cards, you now have 5). Advanced: when setting up a room (or a bot game) you can turn Snap tips off — no banner at the top, just watch the middle yourself. Snapping still works exactly the same.
  • WELLINGTON: on your turn, before drawing, hit the big button when you think you're lowest. Everyone else gets exactly one last turn, then all cards are revealed. Only one call per game — and nobody may touch the caller's cards during the final round. If the caller ties for the lowest score, the caller loses — there is no draw with the caller.
  • Deck runs out? The discard pile (except its top card) is reshuffled into a new draw pile.
  • Joining late? If you join a game that's already in progress, you spectate — you see the table but every card stays face-down, and you join as a player from the next round. If your connection drops mid-game, you have 10 seconds to reconnect and keep your seat; after that your cards go back into the draw pile and you spectate.
